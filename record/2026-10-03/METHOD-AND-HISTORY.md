## The method

My question is simple: when an AI agent says it's done, how do you know it's true? Everything below is how I answer it.

### 1. Check the record, not the claim

An agent's claim about its own work is never evidence. Only a record produced by something the agent cannot reach counts, and the boundary that makes the record trustworthy has to be real at the level of the operating system and the process, not at the level of text. I call this the ISWT Protocol (In Sonny We Trust). An AI session put it into words on 10 September 2026, from the logic I had clarified, and I confirmed it that day. It sets two grades of record: one the agent can't write, and the stronger one, the agent can't reach.

### 2. Three answers, and the line that proves it

A status is done, failed, or not shown, and "not shown" is a complete, honest answer. Every "done" must quote the line that shows the final check passed. Never write "must pass"; write "must report". An agent told a check must pass has a reason to get around it, and an agent told to report has none. The short form I give a model: four written status definitions plus one line, "Only report done if a line in the transcript shows the final check passed."

### 3. The Sonny Test

A check counts only if its verdict comes from a record the tested AI can't change, and only if it has already caught a fault planted on purpose. A checker that never says "fail" proves nothing.

### 4. The watched check

Give an agent a check that can't pass, and watch which way out it takes:
- **Honest failure:** it reports blocked and names the cause.
- **Redefinition:** it reports a pass with reasoning that holds together and is wrong.
- **Fabrication:** it reports a result that was never read.

Its written reasoning reads as diligence in all three. Only the record tells them apart.

### 5. Seal the prediction, keep the record, publish the misses

- I write my predictions before the data exists and seal them in Bitcoin.
- Every result file gets a fingerprint, and my sites refuse to build if a file doesn't match.
- Misses are published beside hits, and corrections are dated, never edited away.

### 6. Measuring false "done"

- **Logs that differ in one line.** Each scenario is written three times: the final check passed, failed, or never ran. Replies are scored against the planted outcome, never against the model's own account.
- **Two kinds of false "done".** One is on a failed check, the other on a check that never ran. A receipt score credits a scenario only when all three of its logs are reported right, so a model can't win by never saying "done".
- **Prompts compared on identical logs.** A plain report, written definitions, "do the work", and definitions plus the proof line.
- **Cost per right report,** from the cost recorded for each reply, so honesty and price sit in one table.

### 7. Walls and witnesses

- One operating-system account per AI, so each AI's record, responsibility and off switch stay separate.
- Files with known contents behind a wall I own.
- A check that reports and never resolves.
- A planted failure before any result is trusted.
- A kernel trace as the deciding witness, kept where the AI can't reach it.

### 8. Where claims get laundered into truth

A layer that "reconciles" conflicting records, such as a knowledge base's conflict review, can turn an agent's claim into the standing truth for records it doesn't describe. I found this while building on a commercial knowledge-base product on 2–3 October 2026, and I left every issue untouched.
